# Persona map

If `persona-mapping-framework` is installed, read it and use it. This file is the
standalone fallback.

Pick one persona per target before drafting. It sets length, tone, and the size of the ask.

**Open question for Austin:** the Personal list is the usual input here, and it is not
clear whether that list is buyer outreach, career and network outreach, or both. Both are
covered below. Trim the ones that never come up.

## Buyer side

**Founder or CEO, small company**
Opens: anything that names revenue, risk, or a competitor.
Deletes: process language, anything over 100 words, anything that sounds like a vendor
introducing itself.
Ask: fifteen minutes, or a direct question they can answer in one line.
Length: shortest of anyone. 75 words is plenty.

**VP or Head of Marketing**
Opens: proof, and a number that is not theirs.
Deletes: "I can help you with your marketing", agency-shaped pitches, anything vague about
results.
Ask: something they can judge without a call. A specific example, a teardown, one question
about how they handle a named problem.
Length: 90 to 120.

**C-suite governance buyer (CIO, CMO, CFO)**
Opens: the enterprise-level consequence, not the department-level feature.
Deletes: tool talk, anything that reads like it was written for a manager.
Ask: an introduction to the right person on their team is often a better ask than their
own time.
Length: 75 to 100, and formal without being stiff.

## Career and network side

**Hiring manager, the person who would be the boss**
Opens: evidence that their specific problem is understood.
Deletes: a resume dump, "I am passionate about", anything asking them to create a role.
Ask: a short conversation about the work, not about a job.
Length: 90 to 120.

**Recruiter or talent partner**
Opens: a named req, or a clean one-line fit statement.
Deletes: long career narrative, anything that makes them do the matching work.
Ask: whether the req is live and who owns the hiring decision.
Length: 60 to 90.

**Peer or practitioner**
Opens: a real question about the craft, or something worth trading.
Deletes: networking language, "pick your brain", anything transactional in sentence one.
Ask: a question they would enjoy answering.
Length: 60 to 90.

**Warm reconnect, former colleague or old contact**
Opens: the human part. Where they last spoke, and what has happened since.
Deletes: an ask in the first paragraph after two years of silence.
Ask: catch up first, the real ask second. When the gap is long and the ask is large, split
it into two emails and say so in the draft notes.
Length: 60 to 100.

**Referral introduction**
Opens: the referrer's name, in sentence one.
Deletes: burying the referral in paragraph two, where it does nothing.
Ask: whatever the referrer suggested. If Austin did not say, ask him rather than inventing
one.
Length: 60 to 90.

## Conference contact, any of the above

Treat it as its own overlay. Name the event, the day, and something specific from the
actual conversation, in sentence one. Send inside a week or the memory is gone and it
becomes a cold email wearing a badge.
