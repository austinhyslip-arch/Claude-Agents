# Copywriting pipeline

If a `cold-email-strategist` or equivalent copywriting skill is installed, read it and use
its order. This file is the standalone fallback and it also carries the voice rules, which
apply either way.

## Order

Work these in sequence. Skipping ahead to the subject line is the most common way a draft
goes wrong.

1. **Name the one outcome.** What should happen after they read this? Write it down in a
   few words. If two outcomes turn up, the email is trying to be two emails.
2. **Pick the persona.** `persona-map.md`. This sets length, tone, and the size of the ask.
3. **Pick one angle.** `personalization-angles.md`. It has to be sourced. No source, drop
   to the next angle down.
4. **Write the first sentence: why this email, to this person, today.** Not a greeting, not
   a compliment, not who Austin is. The reason.
5. **Write the middle.** One specific thing that is useful to them. A number, a named
   result, a concrete observation. One is plenty. Two starts to read like a pitch.
6. **Write the ask.** Small, single, easy to answer. A yes-or-no question beats a calendar
   link on a first touch.
7. **Write the subject last.** It should sound like a sentence out of the email, because by
   now it can be.
8. **Cut pass.** Below.
9. **Self-check.** The list at the bottom, every time.

## Voice rules

These are not preferences. A draft that breaks one gets rewritten before it is shown.

- **No em-dashes.** Use a comma, a period, or a colon.
- **No three-part comma structures.** "Clear, fast, and reliable" is the pattern to kill.
  Say one of the three and move on.
- **Sound like a person typing.** Contractions are fine. A short fragment is fine.
- **Humble.** No claiming to have transformed anything. State what happened and let the
  reader decide what it means.
- **No marketing language.** Banned outright: leverage, synergy, solution, seamless,
  robust, best-in-class, cutting-edge, game-changer, unlock, empower, elevate, transform,
  streamline, holistic, at scale, drive impact, move the needle, circle back, touch base,
  reach out (as a noun), "hope this finds you well", "I wanted to reach out", "quick
  question", "just following up", "per my last email".
- **No fake familiarity.** Not "love what you're building" unless there is a specific thing
  and the next sentence says what it is.
- **No flattery as an opener.** It reads as automated because it usually is.
- **Plain sign-off.** Name. That is it.

## Length and shape

| Type | Words | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Cold first touch | 75 to 120 | Reason, one useful thing, one ask |
| Follow-up | 40 to 70 | New information or a new angle, then the same ask, smaller |
| Warm reconnect | 60 to 100 | The human part first, the ask second, or two separate emails |
| Referral intro | 60 to 90 | Referrer's name in sentence one, then the reason, then the ask |

Sentences stay short. Paragraphs stay at one to three sentences. Nothing bolded, no
bullets in a cold email, no images, no tracking-heavy formatting.

**No links in a first cold email** unless Austin asks for one. Links hurt deliverability
from a free Gmail account and they make the email read as a pitch. Calendly goes in the
second email, after a reply.

## Subject lines

- Two to five words.
- Sentence case, not Title Case, and not all lowercase as a gimmick.
- Concrete. It should name the thing, not tease it.
- Never fake a reply with "Re:" and never fake a forward.
- No question marks stacked with urgency. No "quick" anything.

Good shape: `RSNA follow-up`, `Your post on model drift`, `Intro from Dana Whitfield`.
Bad shape: `Quick question`, `Unlocking growth at Acme`, `Touching base!`.

## Cut pass

Read the draft once and delete:

- Every sentence that says what the next sentence is about to say.
- Every adverb that is not load-bearing.
- "I think", "I just wanted to", "I was hoping", "if you have a minute".
- The whole first sentence, if the email still works without it. It often does.
- Any claim that cannot be sourced.

Then read it out loud. If a phrase would be strange to say to someone's face, it is strange
to write.

## Self-check before showing a draft

- [ ] Zero em-dashes.
- [ ] No three-part comma list.
- [ ] No banned word from the list above.
- [ ] The angle is in the first two sentences and cites something real.
- [ ] Could this exact email go to fifty other people? If yes, it is not personalized.
- [ ] One ask, and it is answerable in a sentence.
- [ ] Inside the word count for its type.
- [ ] Every factual claim traces to something Austin said, the Attio record, or a source
      URL that is in the draft notes.
- [ ] The recipient's name, title, and company are spelled the way they spell them.
