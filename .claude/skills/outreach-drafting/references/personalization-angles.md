# Personalization angles

If `personalization-playbooks` is installed, read it and use it. This file is the
standalone fallback.

One angle per email. It appears in the first two sentences or it is not doing any work.

## The test

Could this exact sentence go to fifty other people? If yes, it is not personalization, it
is a template with a name merged into it. Rewrite or drop to a weaker angle that is
actually true of this one person.

## Ranked, strongest first

**1. Something they said or did, publicly and recently.**
A post, a talk, a quote in an article, a podcast, a hire they announced, a product they
shipped. Strongest because it proves a person read something.
Rule: engage with the substance. "I saw your post" followed by nothing about the post is
worse than no angle at all, because it advertises the automation.
Recency: inside 90 days is safe. Older only if it is still the thing they are known for.

**2. A change at their company that creates the need.**
Funding, a new market, a launch, a leadership change, an acquisition, a reorg, a
regulatory shift in their category. Works when the change plausibly creates the problem
being written about.
Rule: name the change and the consequence in one sentence. Do not narrate their news back
to them, they were there.

**3. A real shared connection.**
A referral, a former colleague, a conference both attended, a mutual client. The strongest
opener in the whole list when the referrer's name can be used.
Rule: confirm with Austin that the referrer is fine being named before naming them.

**4. Something specific and checkable about their role.**
Team size, a stack they publicly use, a job posting they have open, a certification their
category requires. Weaker, but it is still true of this person and not of fifty others.
Rule: an open job posting is a strong version of this one. It is public, dated, and it
says what they are missing.

**5. Nothing.**
No angle survived. Then say plainly why the email is being written and what is wanted, and
keep it under 70 words. An honest short email outperforms a fabricated warm one.

## Sourcing

Every angle carries a source URL into the draft notes, even when the URL never appears in
the email. If it cannot be cited, it does not go in.

Free sources, in order: the company site and its blog or newsroom, the person's public
LinkedIn activity, news search, their podcast or conference appearances, their open job
postings, their changelog or docs. Most angles are found here for nothing.

Note for this environment: direct page fetches to a lot of common domains have been
refused by the network egress policy in past runs. When a fetch is blocked, say so and use
search snippets, and mark the angle as unverified in the draft notes rather than treating
a snippet as confirmed.

## Never

- Never infer an angle from a photo, a name, a location, or anything that reads as
  demographic guessing.
- Never reference something personal that they did not publish themselves.
- Never use a life event (a death, a birth, an illness, a departure that looks involuntary)
  as an opener.
- Never say "I noticed" about something that came out of an enrichment tool rather than
  something a person could plausibly have noticed.
- Never reuse the same angle across two people at the same company.
