# Persona map

If `persona-mapping-framework` is installed, read it and use it. This file is the
standalone fallback.

Pick one persona per target before drafting. It sets length, tone, and the size of the ask.

Both outreach lists are Chanty business outreach, so everything here is a buyer, a
champion, or a route to one. The only difference between the automated list and this one is
who sends the email.

Chanty context: team chat and task management, competing for the same budget as Slack,
Microsoft Teams, ClickUp, Monday.com, Notion and Basecamp. Most buyers already own one of
those, so the email is almost always a switching conversation, not a greenfield one. That
shapes every persona below.

## Founder or owner, small business

Opens: cost, and anything that names a tool they are already paying for.
Deletes: feature lists, anything over 100 words, anything that sounds like a vendor
introducing itself.
Ask: one direct question, or fifteen minutes. Not a demo.
Length: shortest of anyone. 75 words is plenty.
Note: they are the buyer and the user at once, so price and daily annoyance land equally.

## Ops or IT manager

Opens: admin burden, seat management, migration cost, security and access control.
Deletes: growth language, anything implying they should rip out a working system on a whim.
Ask: something concrete they can check without a call. How migration actually works, what
happens to existing history, what the admin surface looks like.
Length: 90 to 120. This is the one persona where a little detail helps.

## Department head or team lead

Opens: the specific friction their team lives with. Threads nobody reads, work tracked in
three places, guests they cannot add without a bill.
Deletes: anything addressed to a buyer, because they usually are not one.
Ask: whether the friction is real for them, and who owns the tooling decision.
Length: 75 to 100.
Note: often the best route to the actual buyer. Asking for the introduction is a legitimate
ask and a better one than asking for their signature.

## HR or internal comms lead

Opens: reaching people who do not sit at a desk, and the parts of the team the current tool
does not cover.
Deletes: engineering-flavored talk, anything about integrations and APIs.
Ask: how they currently reach the people the main tool misses.
Length: 75 to 100.

## Existing user or champion

Opens: something specific about how their team already uses it.
Deletes: anything that reads like it came from a system rather than a person who knows them.
Ask: an introduction, a reference, or feedback. One of the three, not all three.
Length: 60 to 90.
Note: check the record first. `last_interaction` and any synced email will say more than
research can.

## Warm reconnect

A past contact, a stalled conversation, a customer who churned, or someone Austin has met
before.
Opens: the human part. Where they last spoke and what has changed since.
Deletes: an ask in the first paragraph after a long silence.
Ask: catch up first, the real ask second. When the gap is long and the ask is large, split
it into two emails and say so in the draft notes.
Length: 60 to 100.

## Overlays

These sit on top of whichever persona above applies, they do not replace it.

**Referral introduction.** The referrer's name goes in sentence one, not paragraph two
where it does nothing. Confirm with Austin that the referrer is fine being named. The ask
is whatever the referrer suggested, and if Austin did not say, ask rather than inventing one.
Length: 60 to 90.

**Conference contact.** Name the event, the day, and something specific from the actual
conversation, in sentence one. Send inside a week or the memory is gone and it becomes a
cold email wearing a badge.
