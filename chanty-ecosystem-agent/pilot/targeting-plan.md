# Pilot Targeting Plan

About 100 organizations. The purpose is to learn what works, not to build a
database. Nothing here is discovered yet: this is the plan `/eco-discover` works
through, one slice at a time.

## Mix

| Count | Category | Why it is in the pilot |
|---|---|---|
| 20 | Chambers of Commerce | Highest concentration of small employers with real teams, and most run a newsletter and a monthly event, so the distribution mechanism is easy to verify |
| 15 | SCORE and SBDC-type organizations | Trusted educators, existing workshop calendars, no vendor pitch culture, which suits the workshop offer |
| 15 | MSPs and IT consultants | They already choose software for their clients. The referral offer is the natural fit and the economics conversation goes to a human |
| 15 | Professional associations | Members are firms with staff. Verticals with shift or field work matter most |
| 10 | Referral networks (BNI-type) | Weekly meeting cadence, high repetition, low formality |
| 10 | Business coaches and consultants | Small audiences, high trust, fast to test |
| 10 | Vertical associations | Where communication pain is structural: home services, construction, hospitality, property management |
| 5 | High-leverage national organizations | Multi-chapter leverage, all human-led, all Tier A |

## Sequencing

Run one category at a time and finish it before starting the next. Comparing a
half-researched category against a finished one teaches nothing.

Suggested order: chambers, SCORE/SBDC, MSPs, then the rest. The first three cover
the three distinct offer types (workshop, resource, referral), which is what the
test matrix needs.

## The first-pilot rule

The first 20 first-touch drafts land in Austin's Gmail and he reads every one
before anything is sent. Automated outreach stays off until he has done that and
says to turn it on. Audit each for:

- personalization that is actually grounded in evidence
- correct organization classification
- the right contact, not the easiest contact
- an offer that matches a mechanism the organization actually has
- evidence at A or B level behind every specific claim
- tone that sounds like a person
- plain text, no sign-off, no formatting
- any assumption that crept in and got stated as fact

Do not scale until the quality holds. Scaling a broken message just distributes
the damage.

## What we are testing

| Variable | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Offer | Workshop | Resource | Member benefit |
| Trigger | Generic relevance | Timely signal | |
| CTA | Useful question | Meeting request | |
| Contact | Programs | Membership | Partnerships |
| Audience framing | Members | Businesses | Community |

Primary metric: qualified positive response. Secondary: partnership conversion.
Ultimate: paid seats.

## Scale after the pilot

Phase 1: 500-1,000 organizations.
Phase 2: 5,000-10,000 qualified organizations.
Phase 3: continuous national discovery.

Only move to the next phase when the previous one produced partnerships, not
records.
